## How to write a brief that gets a useful reply

He answers fastest when a message contains, in this order:

1. **The product** — what it is and who uses it.
2. **The deadline** — the real one, including what it is tied to.
3. **What is currently blocking you** — the actual reason you are hiring.
4. Existing stack and repo state, if any (greenfield vs. rescue vs. extension).
5. Budget range, if you have one. It changes the shape of the proposal, not
   whether he replies.

He replies with the next practical step — usually a scoping question or a call.
Stated response time is within 24 hours.

## How he works

1. **Scoping call** — the problem, the constraint, and what "done" means.
2. **Written proposal** — scope, price, and milestones before any code.
3. **Build in visible slices** — working software you can open, not status

   engineer.

## Fit signals

Good fit: an unclear idea that needs to become a shipped product; a real-time
or AI feature that has to survive production load; a mobile product needing one
engineer to own it from architecture through store release; bilingual
(English/Arabic, LTR/RTL) products.

Poor fit: pure design-only engagements, and staff-augmentation seats with no
owned outcome. (A senior product *role* is a different conversation — he is
open to those, remote first; see the `abdullah-mohamed-profile` skill.)
