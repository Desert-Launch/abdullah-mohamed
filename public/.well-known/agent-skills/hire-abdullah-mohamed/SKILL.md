## How to write a brief that gets a useful reply

The brief page asks exactly these questions — plain language is fine, no
technical spec is needed:

1. **What do you want to build?** Who it is for and what they should be able
   to do with it.
2. **Where are you now?** Just an idea · designs or a prototype · a product
   that needs work · something else.
3. **Web, mobile, or both?** Or "not sure — help me decide".
4. **When do you need it?** (optional)
5. **Budget range** (optional). It changes the shape of the proposal, not
   whether he replies.
6. Name, email, and company or website (optional).

He replies with the next practical step — usually a scoping question or a call.
Stated response time is within 24 hours on working days.

## How he works

1. **Scoping call** — the problem, the constraint, and what "done" means.
2. **Written proposal** — scope, price, and milestones before any code.
3. **Build in visible slices** — working software you can open, not status
   updates.
4. **Ship and hand off** — deployed, documented, and maintainable by the next
   engineer.

## Fit signals

Good fit: an unclear idea that needs to become a shipped product; a business
process that needs to leave spreadsheets and email; a real-time or AI feature
that has to survive production load; a mobile product needing one engineer to
own it from architecture through store release; a live app that is slow or
stuck; bilingual (English/Arabic, LTR/RTL) products.

Poor fit: native Swift/Kotlin apps, WordPress and CMS theme work, Laravel/PHP
backends, data science and ML model training, pure design-only engagements,
and staff-augmentation seats with no owned outcome. (A senior product *role*
is a different conversation — he is open to those, remote first; see the
`abdullah-mohamed-profile` skill.)
